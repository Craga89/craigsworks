Technical health check

Fixed scope and price, agreed up front. Useful on its own, and the usual place to start.

What the health check covers →

The six areas of a health check, and what I've done in each

  • Pipeline and releases How a change gets from a laptop to customers: CI, environments, testing and the release process. Built Hive's CI/CD from scratch; continuous deployment since 2022.
  • Code and tooling Structure, drift, ageing tools and half-finished migrations. Merged 18 repos into one; led nine repo-wide migrations.
  • Architecture and cost AWS set-up, networking, scaling, and where the bill really goes. Moved to Fargate behind CloudFront and WAF; evidence-led cost cuts.
  • Security and enterprise readiness Access, secrets, public surface, SSO and what auditors and big customers will ask. ISO 27001 and GDPR audits, yearly pen tests, a new auth framework.
  • AI readiness Where AI would help most, and the guardrails it needs first. Led a department from AI-assisted to AI-first in a year.
  • Team and process Ways of working, review, on-call, incidents and documentation. Led teams of up to 12; set up on-call, status page and post-mortems.

How it worked at Hive

One monorepo, built only where it changed

Eighteen repositories became one, with 100+ packages. Each merge builds only what it touched, and ships itself.

  • Build only what changed The pipeline works out which packages a change affects, and builds those rather than everything.
  • Every merge ships itself No release train since 2022. Merged work reaches customers on its own.
  • A bad deploy rolls itself back Every deploy checks it's healthy, so nobody has to sit with it.
  • Reviewed by AI and people Every pull request rated for risk before a person opens it.

Things people ask

Who do you usually work with?

Startups and small-to-medium companies with a product already in customers' hands, that want to ship faster or get more from AI without letting quality slip. Usually there's an engineering team in place and I work alongside it.

Do you write code, or just advise?

Both. I'm still hands-on day to day, so I'll happily change the pipeline or pair on the first pull requests myself. The aim is that your team can carry it on afterwards.

How much does it cost?

The health check is a fixed price agreed before we start, based on the size of what we're looking at. Anything hands-on is scoped and priced the same way, piece by piece.

We're not sure we're ready for AI. Is that a problem?

Not at all. Plenty of teams get more from faster releases first. When you do want to bring AI in, the same groundwork (clear standards, good tests, quick feedback) is what makes it work.

How much of your time can we have?

I take on a small number of projects at a time so each one gets proper attention. We'll agree what that looks like up front.

Do you work remotely?

Yes, remote by default, from Newcastle upon Tyne in the UK.
