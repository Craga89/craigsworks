The problems I'm usually asked about

These are the ones I've lived with and worked through myself, so the fixes come from experience rather than a playbook.

  • Releases are an event

    Changes wait for a release day, and someone has to sit with each deploy in case it goes wrong.

    what's worked before At Hive we stopped having a release train in 2022. Merged work reaches customers by itself, and a bad deploy rolls itself back.

  • CI is the slowest part of the day

    Every change rebuilds everything, and flaky tests get re-run until they pass.

    what's worked before Builds that work out what a change touched and build only that, and a test suite moved to Playwright for speed and stability.

  • You've tried AI, with mixed results

    Some of the team swear by it, some don't trust it, and nobody agrees what good AI-written code looks like.

    what's worked before Coding guidelines kept in the repo, followed by people and models alike, and an AI review that rates every pull request before a person opens it.

  • The codebase has drifted

    Old tooling, half-finished migrations, and several repos that really should be one.

    what's worked before 18 repositories merged into one with their history intact, then nine repo-wide migrations, each landed as a single reviewed change.

  • Bigger customers ask harder questions

    Security questionnaires, single sign-on, pen tests, ISO 27001. Each deal brings a new list.

    what's worked before A platform taken to enterprise-ready: SAML SSO, 2FA per organisation, a yearly pen test, and ISO 27001 and GDPR audits every year.

  • The cloud bill keeps creeping up

    It's grown with the product, and nobody is quite sure what's driving it.

    what's worked before Measure first, then cut: about $1.3k a month from the first three changes, each with the evidence in its pull request.

Who do you usually work with?

Startups and small-to-medium companies with a product already in customers' hands, that want to ship faster or get more from AI without letting quality slip. Usually there's an engineering team in place and I work alongside it.

Do you write code, or just advise?

Both. I'm still hands-on day to day, so I'll happily change the pipeline or pair on the first pull requests myself. The aim is that your team can carry it on afterwards.

How much does it cost?

The health check is a fixed price agreed before we start, based on the size of what we're looking at. Anything hands-on is scoped and priced the same way, piece by piece.

We're not sure we're ready for AI. Is that a problem?

Not at all. Plenty of teams get more from faster releases first. When you do want to bring AI in, the same groundwork (clear standards, good tests, quick feedback) is what makes it work.

How much of your time can we have?

I take on a small number of projects at a time so each one gets proper attention. We'll agree what that looks like up front.

Do you work remotely?

Yes, remote by default, from Newcastle upon Tyne in the UK.
